2. COMPUTATIONAL ENVIRONMENT & LIMITATION DISCLOSURE
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
  Hardware Environment    : Windows CPU execution (no GPU acceleration).
  System Constraints      : PyTorch restricted by OS Application Control policy;
                            tensorflow_text unavailable on Windows / Python 3.13;
                            Keras Hub Kaggle preset downloads unavailable.
  Execution Strategy      : Fully loaded pretrained weights from local safetensors
                            into Keras Hub DistilBertBackbone with fast tokenizers.
  CRITICAL LIMITATION     : Full fine-tuning on 102,076 samples on CPU would require
                            approx. 14 hours per epoch (~42 hours for 3 epochs).
                            To complete Phase 7 within the strict 2-hour window,
                            a stratified sample of 10,000 training instances was
                            fine-tuned for 1 epoch, evaluated on the entire full validation
                            (5,420 samples) and full test (6,530 samples) sets.

6. RESEARCH QUESTIONS & SYSTEMATIC ANALYSIS (Q1 — Q7)
--------------------------------------------------------------------------------
Q1: Does pretrained DistilBERT improve over the scratch BiLSTM?
A1: DistilBERT achieved a Test Macro F1 of 0.5697 (Accuracy: 0.5769) versus the Phase 5 scratch
    BiLSTM Test Macro F1 of 0.6557 (Accuracy: 0.6579). However, this comparison is not
    equivalent: DistilBERT was trained on a restricted 10,000-sample stratified subset for
    only 1 epoch due to CPU computational constraints, whereas the BiLSTM was trained on
    the full 102,076 samples for 3 epochs.

Q2: Does it exceed the Phase 4 benchmark Macro F1 = 0.6657?
A2: NO. The Phase 4 benchmark (TF-IDF + 12 scaled linguistic features + Logistic Regression,
    trained on all 102,076 samples) achieved Macro F1 = 0.6657. DistilBERT reached 0.5697
    (Delta: -0.0960). Phase 4 holds the top benchmark because it combined the
    complete training corpus with hand-crafted sentiment signals (negation counts, punctuation,
    casing, and intensifiers), while DistilBERT was limited to 10,000 samples on CPU.

Q3: How do negative/neutral/positive class F1 scores compare?
A3: Per-class performance on the test set:
    - Negative : Precision=0.5930, Recall=0.4919, F1=0.5378
    - Neutral  : Precision=0.6459, Recall=0.4757, F1=0.5479
    - Positive : Precision=0.5384, Recall=0.7407, F1=0.6235
    Positive sentiment achieved the highest F1 score (0.6235) driven by high recall (0.7407).
    Consistent with previous phases, neutral classification remained challenging, with
    the lowest recall (0.4757) among all three categories.

Q4: What does pretrained representation provide compared with scratch embeddings?
A4: Scratch embeddings (Phase 5) suffer from cold-start semantic representations, capturing
    only surface co-occurrences within the training corpus and failing to capture complex polysemy
    or subtle linguistic shifts. Pretrained DistilBERT provides deep contextualized representations
    trained on massive English corpora (BookCorpus + English Wikipedia). Each token embedding
    is dynamically conditioned on its surrounding 128-token bidirectional context via multi-head
    self-attention, capturing negation scope, syntactic dependencies, and nuance effortlessly.

Q5: Is there evidence of overfitting from train/validation history?
A5: Train loss was 1.0228 (accuracy 0.4801) while validation loss was 0.9440 (accuracy 0.5603).
    No clear evidence of overfitting is visible within the single recorded epoch, but one
    epoch is insufficient to assess longer-term overfitting or validation divergence.

Q6: What is the computational cost?
A6: DistilBERT contains 66,955,779 parameters across 6 transformer layers. On CPU:
    - Training throughput   : ~2.88 samples/sec (total train time: 57m 49s)
    - Inference throughput  : ~6.04 samples/sec (total test eval: 18m 0s)
    - Full dataset training on CPU (102,076 samples for 3 epochs) would require ~42 hours,
      making CPU fine-tuning on massive corpora impractical without GPU acceleration.
    - In contrast, Phase 4 (TF-IDF + LR) trained in under 2 minutes with high throughput.

Q7: Overall findings, limitations, and conclusion.
A7: Key findings:
    1. Feasibility & Implementation: Phase 7 is a constrained feasibility experiment
       because it used a 10,000-sample training subset and 1 epoch on CPU. It fully
       demonstrated that local cached HuggingFace DistilBERT safetensors can be cleanly
       reconstructed and executed in native Keras 3 / TensorFlow 2.21 without requiring
       blocked PyTorch or unavailable Kaggle downloads.
    2. Benchmark Comparison: Among the completed experiments, Phase 4 achieved the
       highest Macro F1 (0.6657).
    3. Computational Reality: Pretrained transformer fine-tuning on CPU without GPU
       acceleration entails high computational latency (~2.88 training samples/sec),
       making complete full-dataset multi-epoch training prohibitive within standard
       interactive timelines without hardware acceleration.
